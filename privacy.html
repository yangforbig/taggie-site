The short version

Taggie is an independently developed personal reading app for iOS, currently in private beta. You can read locally without an account. Optional account and archive features use Supabase. Imported reading files are not a public catalogue.

This notice covers the app, its optional Taggie cloud service, and this informational website. It describes the current beta, not a promise that every planned feature is available.

What is processed, and why

ContextData and purpose
On your deviceImported originals, generated EPUB/Markdown and images, titles, authors, source links, library records, reading position, preferences, highlights, notes and reflections support reading and your personal library.
Optional accountEmail, display name, account ID, authentication credentials and provider identity information support sign-in and account access. Supabase Auth processes email/password authentication or supported browser-based provider sign-in. The app stores its archive session in the iOS Keychain.
Optional archiveUploaded original and derived files, source metadata, canonical text blocks, ordered media, content fingerprints, ownership records and upload/retry records support private file storage, edition matching and recovery. Supabase provides authentication, database, private storage and backend processing.
Comments and repliesSelected quotations, text anchors, your writing, author display information, audience and reply relationships support annotation features. The backend supports private and shared annotations; the current native app keeps ordinary private notes and closeout reflections locally. Complete note/reflection recovery across devices is not implemented.

Internal edition matching is not permission to read another person's files. A shared comment is visible to eligible readers of the matching edition and includes its selected quotation and author information. It does not publish your entire book or private reflection. Choose shared comments deliberately.

Network requests and other services

Article acquisition is separate from sign-in

When you convert a supported X article, the app sends its status/article identifier to the third-party FxTwitter API at api.fxtwitter.com and downloads supported images from pbs.twimg.com. Those services receive your network request, including your IP address and requested resource. Conversion runs on your device; the source response and generated files are saved locally and may be included in an optional private archive upload. This route currently does not require an X account or use X sign-in tokens.

Optional browser-based authentication

Supported OAuth sign-in, such as Google, opens a system browser authentication session through Supabase. The provider and Supabase process identity and authorization information under their own policies; Taggie receives account and provider identity information used to verify its archive session.

X sign-in and account linking are not active. Their code is implemented but disabled and has not passed live sign-in/linking verification. The current Supabase X provider defaults may request users.email, users.read, tweet.read and offline.access. Identity-only consent is not enforced or verified. Activation requires further review and approval; this notice does not represent that broader access is already enabled.

Provider account-linking behavior may also depend on Supabase's server-side verified-email policies. Taggie does not promise all linking can occur only through an explicit app linking action.

This website

These static pages contain no analytics, advertising, tracking scripts, external fonts, account forms or app-library uploads. There is no site JavaScript and these pages do not set application cookies. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub and its delivery infrastructure may process visitor IP addresses, requested URLs and other ordinary connection/log information to deliver and operate the site. This does not mean the hosting provider collects no data.

Relevant provider policies: Supabase, Google, X, and GitHub. FxTwitter is an independent third-party acquisition service, not the official X API; availability and its handling of request data are outside Taggie's control.

Your choices, storage and deletion

Use the local library without creating an account, and do not upload files if you do not want them processed by the archive service. Sign-out does not itself delete your account, cloud files or local library. A private archive is not end-to-end encrypted storage; this beta does not make an end-to-end encryption guarantee.

Keep independent copies of important originals and writing. Current cloud restore supports your EPUB files, not a complete multi-device snapshot of notes, reflections and reading state. New-user email onboarding and full recovery remain under validation.

Local data persists in the app's device storage until removed. Cloud data may persist until removed through supported service operations or an operator-assisted request. There is no published fixed retention deadline or verified complete self-service account-deletion flow. Backups, provider logs and operational records may have separate lifecycles; immediate erasure from every copy is not promised.

For access, correction or deletion requests about account/cloud data, use the contact below. Please describe the request and the account email if relevant; do not send passwords or tokens. Identity verification may be needed before action. Requests are handled with the service providers as applicable, subject to technical and legal limitations. Local-only writing may not be accessible to the operator.
